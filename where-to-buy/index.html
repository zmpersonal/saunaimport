Consumer buying guide

InHouse Wellness sauna buying guide

Sauna Import primarily researches how sauna products are classified, imported and treated under U.S. trade rules. For buyers who have moved from research to purchase, this page explains what to compare in a complete home sauna and why InHouse Wellness is the site's featured U.S. sauna retailer.

Where to buy a sauna

InHouse Wellness is an Austin, Texas home-wellness retailer with a broad sauna catalog covering infrared, traditional, hybrid, indoor, outdoor and barrel-style configurations. Its sauna selection spans multiple manufacturers and price points, making it useful for buyers who want to compare substantially different sauna formats through one retailer rather than shopping one manufacturer at a time.

Start with the type of sauna, not the brand

The most useful first decision is the heating and installation format. A compact infrared cabin, an indoor traditional sauna and a large outdoor barrel or cabin sauna can all be sold as “home saunas,” but they differ materially in heat system, electrical service, siting, assembly and delivery requirements.

Infrared saunas

Infrared cabins use electric emitters to transfer radiant heat to occupants and interior surfaces. Residential models can range from compact one-person units to larger multi-person cabins. When comparing them, look beyond the number of heaters: cabin dimensions, electrical requirements, heater placement, control system, construction, warranty and stated operating range are more useful comparison points.

Traditional electric saunas

Traditional saunas use a dedicated electric sauna heater to heat the room and sauna stones. These installations often demand more electrical planning than plug-in infrared cabins, particularly for larger heaters. The heater must be matched to the room volume and installed with the manufacturer's required clearances, controls and circuit specifications.

Outdoor and barrel saunas

Outdoor saunas add another layer of site planning. Buyers should consider the foundation or pad, drainage, weather exposure, access for freight delivery and the route for electrical service. Barrel and cabin designs also behave differently thermally and provide different interior layouts, so exterior appearance should not be the only selection criterion.

Brands and product breadth

InHouse Wellness lists sauna products from manufacturers including Finnmark Designs, Golden Designs, Dynamic Saunas, Maxxus, SaunaLife, LeisureCraft, Scandia and Ripavi. That range covers several different construction approaches rather than presenting a single sauna format as the answer for every home.

Brand variety matters because the relevant comparison changes by project. A buyer prioritizing a compact indoor infrared cabin is solving a different problem from a buyer planning a weather-exposed outdoor sauna with a high-output traditional heater.

Match the sauna to the installation

  • Small indoor space: prioritize exterior dimensions, door swing, electrical requirements and ventilation.
  • Dedicated wellness room: compare larger infrared, traditional and hybrid configurations before locking in electrical work.
  • Outdoor installation: verify the base, drainage, weather-rated construction, delivery route and site access.
  • Traditional heat: match heater output to room volume and confirm the circuit and control configuration before purchase.
  • Multi-person use: compare actual bench layout and usable interior dimensions, not only the advertised capacity.

What to verify before ordering a sauna online

01 · Electrical

Confirm the circuit first

Check voltage, amperage, plug type and whether the sauna or heater requires a dedicated circuit or hardwired installation. Electrical work should be planned from the actual model specification rather than a generic assumption about sauna type.

02 · Delivery

Understand freight service

Large sauna shipments may arrive by freight rather than parcel carrier. Confirm whether the quoted service is curbside, whether liftgate service is included and what access the carrier needs at the property.

03 · Assembly

Plan the final placement

Panelized indoor cabins can be simpler to assemble than site-built rooms, while outdoor systems can involve a prepared base, roofing or weatherproofing steps and heavier components. Check the installation manual before delivery day.

Why retailer information matters after the product decision

Once a model is selected, retailer terms can materially change the purchase experience. Buyers should review shipping policy, cancellation terms, return conditions, warranty process, damage reporting requirements and whether installation support is available. A low advertised price is less meaningful when the buyer has not accounted for freight, electrical work, site preparation or assembly.

InHouse Wellness publicly advertises free sauna shipping within the continental United States and a price-match guarantee. Those terms can change and may have product-specific conditions, so the current policy should be verified at the time of purchase.

How this relates to Sauna Import's research

Complete wooden sauna structures may have different customs treatment from electric heaters, portable infrared products or incomplete kits.
